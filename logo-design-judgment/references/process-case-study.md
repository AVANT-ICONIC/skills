# Worked Example: Iron Grip (full client process)

From the shared course video (l9_BM1opTj8, full-process chapter 49:40-57:46; same project as playlist item 10). A new jiu-jitsu school arrived with no logo and no name. The sequence shows how each phase feeds the next.

1. **Client interview first.** Situation, goals, pain points, audience - before any visual work (49:55-50:30).
2. **Keywords from the client**, filtered for audience resonance: jiu-jitsu, teaching, team, strong, community (50:30-52:05). The audience wants belonging and to become stronger; the brand should express robust strength.
3. **Study the industry until fluent** - terminology, culture, existing visual language. Moodboards showed bold sans-serif type and strong geometry across the niche, ruling out serif formality (51:06-51:45).
4. **Expand keywords into related words, then pair them**, thinking visually about each pair (52:30-53:30). The brand name itself came out of a pair: *Iron Grip* - "iron" (strength, metal) + "grip" (core jiu-jitsu element).
5. **Concept generation around the name** (53:30-56:00). First idea: a fist - honest start, but cliche and too detailed, so it was rebuilt as simplified geometry. Second concept: the G of Grip redrawn as a side-view fist with a metallic hook - a small detail that gives the pitch a story.
6. **Shape psychology applied deliberately**: straight edges and angles for strength and stability; a circular enclosure to carry team/togetherness (54:50-56:10).
7. **Fix composition problems with variants**: too much negative space above the wordmark produced three layout solutions (56:10-56:40).
8. **The client chose the triangle variant** - not the designer's favorite. The process informs; the client decides (56:40-57:46).

Transferable pattern: research narrows the space; keywords and word-pairs generate concepts; shape psychology selects forms; geometry executes; variants solve composition; testing and the client close.
