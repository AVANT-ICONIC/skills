# Progression, encounters, world coherence and variation

The world and player must evolve **together**. Progression that only raises numbers while choices remain identical often makes a game feel like chores with better graphics.

## D13: Level and encounter blueprint planner

Map early, midpoint and advanced encounters against the skills taught and the choice the player must make. The first encounter proves controls and feedback; the second tests a changed resource or spatial constraint; the later encounter demands a new combination or recognition, not just more health. Show layout/camera implications: where does the player stand, what is readable, which controls are needed, and what creates a decision rather than an unavoidable animation?

**Encounter mini-blueprint:** First: a single route must be opened. Second: two simultaneous routes compete for a limited beam. Third: unstable terrain changes route costs while a scarce emergency tool is available. Same basic verb, different pressure and player learning; no obligation to invent boss fights.

## D10: Progression's economic dependencies

Distinguish permanent unlock, consumable, temporary buff, information unlock and systemic world evolution. For each milestone specify trigger, cost, feedback, retained knowledge and changed decision space. Trace early progress into later challenge so the advanced player doesn't simply execute the same action faster. Reset/prestige belongs only where loss of old capabilities generates meaningful new objectives.

**Progression audit:** If an upgrade makes every other upgrade irrelevant, diagnose the dominant strategy. If later levels need only ten more clicks, revise the combination of constraints rather than invent an arbitrary new currency.

## D14: World and lore consistency across time

Changes must persist or reset according to explicit rules: where do removed materials go, why does a threatened location recover, what does the local population learn, and which assets or routes remain? If entities reproduce, decay, migrate, flee or cooperate, these dynamics should affect traversal, scarcity, strategy and feedback. Clearly state when an implication is abstracted for scope; not every world needs a detailed ecological simulation.

**Countercheck:** A permanently drained pond should not be full in the next level without an identified reset/rain/season rule if water scarcity is central to the game.

## Variation designer

Use systemic perturbations rather than random skins: new layout constraints, weather modifiers, opponent tactics, alternative tools, altered information, player-made structures and market shifts. A new enemy/species/tool changes the loop only if some old strategy now fails and a different one becomes attractive. For cooperative games, variation should alter meaningful role interdependence, not simply increase simultaneous clicking.

**Five-minute test:** Write three consecutive challenges. Identify the reason the player changes their plan in each. If all three lead to the same opening action, variation has not earned its cost. Keep a minimal core before proposing procedural generators, persistent worlds or live-service complexity.
