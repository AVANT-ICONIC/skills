# Framing, coherence, vision and creative constraints

Treat this as a causal design process, not four separate interview rounds. The smallest usable output is a coherent *what the player does + why a choice matters* hypothesis. Audience, fantasy and resources set constraints, but those constraints must be tested against inputs and feedback, not decided in isolation.

## D00: Design coherence engine

Build a dependency sketch: **promise → verbs → rules → feedback → tradeoff → progression → presentation → production cost**. For each major proposed system, annotate the effect on at least one neighboring system. When a rule changes, revisit affected resources, challenges, UI messages, world logic, upgrades and tests. Explicitly identify contradictory cases (e.g., infinite energy and “scarcity” as the central challenge) and reject incoherent combinations, even when a single component sounds exciting.

**Worked micro-check:** If the player permanently extends a bridge, the world should change traversal routes, enemy/traffic options, upgrade value and failure risks. If nothing besides a sprite changes, the proposed feature is not mechanical progression. Preserve the old coherent design if a new feature cannot be reconciled.

## D01: Game vision architect

State: “The player is a [role] who [controllable verb] to [achieve changing goal] while [distinct pressure].” Choose platform/view/camera based on required physical interactions, not the other way round. Explain who benefits from the pacing (short mobile sessions versus deep desktop runs) without inventing market validation. Use a single representative decision to prove the fantasy is performed rather than observed.

**Acceptance probe:** After 30 seconds, a reader should identify the player role, button/tap, pressure and reason to repeat, with no lore dump. If the pitch says “be the pilot” but only menus are clickable, revise either the promise or controls.

## D02: Design pillars architect

Use two or three **testable design pillars** and at least one anti-pillar. A pillar must influence a choice, such as “every new route closes another” rather than “beautiful world.” Tag essential mechanics with a pillar and explicit player benefit. For a conflicting feature, simplify/remove it or make a documented user-facing tradeoff. Pillars are constraints on production and experience, not mandatory headings in every concept answer.

**Acceptance probe:** In a proposed upgrade list, the designer can remove a flashy unrelated ability without breaking the central game. If every feature is “essential,” pillars are not doing their job.

## D05: High concept and elevator pitch (first-pass integration)

Write a one-line hook with a *concrete player verb*, a dynamic obstacle and the resulting fantasy. Then verify this exact promise against the first-minute control mapping, actual rules and longer progression. A compelling sentence is not proof of enjoyment. Reject slogans made only of setting names, “unique blend” adjectives or arbitrary genre stacking.

**Counterexample:** “Run a civilization of living glass” is not enough; “Rotate three mirrors to choose which district receives daylight while shadows consume the others” proposes an actual repeated dilemma.

## D20: Scope and feature prioritizer (concept stage)

Separate **irreducible loop**, **experimentally necessary feedback**, and **deferred ambition**. The first playable test should contain only enough systems to force alternatives and consequences. For every proposed secondary feature ask: “What false-positive fun verdict would its absence create?” If the answer is none, defer it. Do not prematurely cap creativity; sketch the audacious vision and isolate its smallest falsifiable mechanic.

**Acceptance probe:** The scope list contains at least one deliberate cut with why it won't invalidate the fun test. It does not promise multiplayer, procedural narrative or a persistent economy merely because those would be possible someday.
