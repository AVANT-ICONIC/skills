# Systems, constrained economies and balancing

A game economy is a system of *player opportunity costs*, not a currency icon. Apply only as deep as the concept needs; abstract puzzle games may have spatial/time economies without coins.

## D10: Economy and progression designer

Inventory sources, sinks, stock limits, conversion rates, travel/time costs and recovery opportunities. Specify when a resource is created, spent, lost, exchanged, saved or reset; avoid infinite arbitrage and unbounded exponential growth without corrective pressure. For each upgrade ask which **verb, risk threshold, route or option** it changes, rather than only “+10% output.” Progression can be new information, tool combinations, reach, timing or enemy adaptation, not necessarily a shop.

**Economy stress test:** Suppose a salvage mission grants 5 parts; an upgrade costs 10 and saves 3 parts per next mission. Show exactly when it pays back and whether it simply becomes a universally optimal first purchase. Introduce competing commitments or constraints if that choice is automatic, and do not pretend it has been numerically tuned without simulation.

## D09: Cross-system interaction and feedback

Tie scarcity to world response: extracting from one location may reduce repairs there, create hazards elsewhere and change available path choices. Track lagged effects and loops, not just direct reward. Distinguish a true emergent response (changed accessible options) from random new flavor. Record how extra actors modify encounter dynamics and the player's existing choices.

**Interaction acceptance:** Change one system variable and trace two downstream player-facing effects and one possible counteraction. If nothing beyond score changes, connect or remove the system.

## D11: Choice balance under circumstances

List two to four viable candidate strategies with state-dependent payoff, cost and counter. Describe the information available when choosing and whether the decision can be reversed. Consider optimistic players, risk-averse players and players who find exploits. Check whether combining upgrades eliminates all challenges, whether one opening always dominates and whether difficulty changes only by damage inflation.

**Balance drill:** Safe routes drain time; risky shortcuts drain gear. Each should be better for a different current world state. If the optimal route never flips, this is a false dichotomy. Simulate edge cases on a cheap spreadsheet or simple deterministic script when actually authorized and the task warrants it, recording assumptions and input values.

## D14: World logic and lore consistency (resource implications)

Ask where energy, food, actors, knowledge and danger originate and where they go. If a faction can infinitely duplicate resources but lacks equipment, identify a believable limiting constraint or accept the world contradiction as an intentional fiction and communicate it. Treat species, weather, politics, machines and ecosystems as *rule generators* that affect player opportunities, not merely description generators.

**World falsifier:** Introduce a new type of transporter; re-evaluate transport bottlenecks, prices, encounter routes and player strategies. A lore addition that cannot have plausible systemic impact should not receive priority over the core loop.
